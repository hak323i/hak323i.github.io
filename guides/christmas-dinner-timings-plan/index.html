Christmas dinner timings: a simple countdown plan for a 2pm dinner

Christmas Day falls on a Friday this year, 25 December 2026. The cooking itself isn't hard. What catches people out is the timing: one oven, a big bird and six things that all need to be hot at once. The trick is to start from the time you want to eat and work backwards. Here's how, with a worked example.

Step 1: work out the turkey first

Everything else fits around the bird, so start there. Always check the cooking and defrosting instructions on the packaging first. If there aren't any, the Food Standards Agency's Christmas food hygiene guidance gives these times for an unstuffed turkey in an oven preheated to 180°C (160°C fan, Gas Mark 4):

Turkey weightCooking time
Under 4.5 kg45 minutes per kg, plus 20 minutes
4.5 kg to 6.5 kg40 minutes per kg
Over 6.5 kg35 minutes per kg

The same guidance says to cook the stuffing in a separate tin rather than inside the bird, not to wash raw turkey, and to check it's cooked all the way through before serving: steaming hot, no pink meat and juices running clear.

Step 2: don't forget the defrost

A frozen turkey needs days, not hours. The FSA says to defrost it in the fridge, on the bottom shelf in a covered container that catches drips, and to allow around 10 to 12 hours per kg. A 6 to 7 kg bird can take as much as four days. Don't defrost it at room temperature.

So for a 5 kg turkey you need 50 to 60 hours in the fridge. To have it ready for the oven on Christmas morning, it needs to move from the freezer to the fridge by Tuesday 22 December at the latest. Write that date on the calendar now.

Step 3: worked example for a 2pm dinner

Here's a 5 kg unstuffed turkey, which works out at 5 × 40 = 200 minutes, or 3 hours 20 minutes. Your own times will differ, so swap in your numbers.

TimeWhat happens
9:15amTake the turkey out of the fridge, oven on to preheat, prep the tin
9:45amTurkey in the oven
11:30amPeel anything not done the day before; get the potatoes parboiled and roughed up
1:05pmTurkey out (check it's cooked through), cover loosely with foil to rest. Turn the oven up for the potatoes
1:10pmRoast potatoes in; stuffing and pigs in blankets in when there's room
1:30pmVeg on the hob; make the gravy with the resting juices
1:50pmCarve; warm the plates in the switched-off oven
2:00pmDinner on the table

The resting time is what makes this work. While the turkey rests, the oven is free for the potatoes and trimmings, which is how one oven copes.

What you can do the day before

  • Peel and chop the veg and keep it covered in the fridge.
  • Make the stuffing, the bread sauce and the cranberry sauce.
  • Set the table and find the serving dishes, carving knife and foil.
  • Write the timings on one sheet and stick it on the fridge, so anyone helping can see what's next.

Leftovers

The FSA advises cooling cooked food and getting it into the fridge or freezer within one to two hours. Splitting it into smaller portions helps it cool faster. See the full FSA guidance for more.
