How to 3D print a desk stand for a wireless thermostat

Lots of wireless room thermostats come with a little wall plate and nothing else, so they end up flat on a shelf or lost in a drawer. A desk stand fixes that: the screen tilts towards you and the thermostat stays put. If you've got a 3D printer, it's an easy, cheap print. Here's how to get one that fits first time.

Step 1: measure your thermostat

The only number that really matters is the thickness of the thermostat body, front to back. Measure it with a ruler or calipers at the bottom edge, where it will sit in the stand. Write it down in millimetres.

My Wave stand fits units roughly 16 to 36 mm thick, using four swappable spacer bars (20, 24, 28 and 32 mm). Pick the bar closest to your measurement. If the thermostat is loose, try the next one up; if it won't go in, go down one.

The full plate (stand plus all four spacer bars) is about 92 g and about 2 hours in the slicer. That's a slicer estimate rather than a timed print, so your own numbers may differ a little. It fits on a 256 × 256 mm bed.

Step 3: two colours or one

The front lettering on the Wave stand is a separate part. Print it in a second colour for contrast, or print the whole thing in one colour if you'd rather keep it simple. A plain front is included too.

Where to put it

A room thermostat reads the temperature around it, so where it sits changes how your heating behaves. Check your thermostat's manual for the maker's advice, but in general:

  • Keep it away from radiators, fires, ovens and other heat sources.
  • Avoid sunny windowsills and spots in direct sunlight.
  • Avoid draughty spots by doors and windows.
  • Put it in a room you actually use, at roughly the height you sit or stand.

There's a bonus: PLA can soften when it gets hot, so keeping it off sunny windowsills and away from radiators is good for the stand as well.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring the wrong part. Measure the bottom edge that sits in the stand, not the thickest point of a curved back.
  • Printing the spacers in the wrong orientation. The ready-made 3MF plates are already laid out, so start from those if you're unsure.
  • Printing too hot or too fast. If the curves look rough, slow the outer walls down a little.
