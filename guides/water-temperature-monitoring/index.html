Monthly water temperature checks: sentinel outlets explained

Temperature control is the most common way of controlling legionella in hot and cold water systems. Here's how routine checks work and how to record them clearly.

Why temperature matters

Legionella bacteria grow best between roughly 20°C and 45°C. Keeping hot water hot and cold water cold, and stopping water from sitting still, are the main controls described in HSE's ACOP L8 and HSG274 Part 2.

What is a sentinel outlet?

Sentinel outlets are the taps nearest to and furthest from the hot water source or cold water tank. If water reaches the right temperature there, the rest of the system is probably fine. Many sites also rotate checks around other outlets so that every outlet is checked over the course of a year.

The targets

WhereTargetTiming
Hot water leaving the cylinder or calorifierAt least 60°CMonthly
Hot water returning to the calorifierAt least 50°CMonthly
Hot sentinel outletsAt least 50°C (55°C in healthcare)Within 1 minute, monthly
Cold sentinel outletsBelow 20°CWithin 2 minutes, monthly

Outlets fitted with thermostatic mixing valves (TMVs) deliver blended water at a lower temperature by design. Check the supply to the valve instead, and service TMVs as your risk assessment says.

How to take a reading

  1. Use a calibrated digital thermometer with a probe.
  2. Run the outlet and start timing.
  3. Hold the probe in the flow and note the temperature at the required time.
  4. Record the date, location, reading, time taken and your initials.
  5. If the reading fails, note it, report it to the responsible person and record what was done.

Weekly flushing of little-used outlets

Outlets that aren't used at least weekly, such as spare bathrooms, empty flats or rarely used showers, should be flushed weekly until the water reaches a steady temperature. Log each flush. If an outlet is never needed, consider removing it together with its dead leg of pipework.

Common failures and what they usually mean

  • Hot outlet slow to reach 50°C: long pipe runs, poor insulation or a balancing problem.
  • Cold outlet above 20°C: heat gain from nearby hot pipes, a warm tank room or low use.
  • Calorifier below 60°C: check the thermostat setting and the heating controls.
