Printable emotion and sensory break cards: how to use them well

Feelings cards help someone show how they're feeling without having to find the words in the moment. A break card lets them ask for a pause before things get too much. Both are simple to make, but a little planning makes the difference between cards that live in a drawer and cards that genuinely help.

Feelings check-in cards

Start small

Begin with three or four feelings: happy, sad, angry and worried cover a lot. Add others, such as tired, excited, frustrated or calm, once the first ones are familiar. Every card should have a clear face and a word.

Make it routine, not only for hard moments

A check-in at the same times each day, such as after school or at registration, makes the cards ordinary. If they only come out when someone's upset, they can start to feel like a warning sign.

Model it yourself

"I'm feeling tired today," pointing at the card, shows that everyone has feelings and that naming them is normal.

Accept the answer

If someone picks "angry", thank them for telling you. You don't need to talk them out of it. Ask, or offer a card for, what might help.

Sensory break cards

A break card is a way to say "I need a break" without words. It works best when it's agreed in advance:

  1. Agree what a break looks like. Where can they go, what can they do and roughly how long is it?
  2. Keep the card within reach, on a desk, a keyring or a lanyard.
  3. Honour it every time, especially at first. Trust is what makes it work.
  4. Make returning easy. A timer or a "back to..." card helps the transition back.

Ideas for break activities

  • Quiet corner or a beanbag
  • Headphones or ear defenders
  • A walk to get a drink of water
  • Something to squeeze or fidget with
  • Movement: jumping, a lap of the playground, carrying something heavy
  • Looking at a favourite book

Everyone's sensory preferences are different. Some people seek movement or noise; others need less. Ask the person, watch what helps, and change the options to suit.

Example: a classroom setup

WhatWhereHow it's used
Feelings strip (4 faces)On the deskPoint at the start of each lesson
Break cardOn a lanyardShown to the teacher; five-minute break in the reading corner
Choice board (3 break options)In the reading cornerChoose what to do during the break
"Back to class" cardNext to a sand timerReturn when the timer finishes

Share the same cards and words with home if you can, so the person doesn't have to learn two systems.
