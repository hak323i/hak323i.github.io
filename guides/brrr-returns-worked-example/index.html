How to calculate BRRR returns: a UK worked example

BRRR stands for buy, refurbish, refinance, rent. The aim is to add value, refinance at the higher value and pull most of your cash back out. Here's how to work out whether a deal really stacks up.

The key numbers

  • Total cash in: purchase price, stamp duty, legal and survey fees, refurbishment and holding costs.
  • Refinance proceeds: the new mortgage at a loan-to-value (LTV) on the end value, minus fees.
  • Money left in: total cash in minus refinance proceeds.
  • Annual cash flow: rent minus all running costs, including mortgage interest.
  • Return on money left in: annual cash flow ÷ money left in.

Worked example

These figures are illustrative only. They assume a purchase in England by someone who already owns a home, so the 5% higher rates of stamp duty apply (see our stamp duty guide). For simplicity they also assume a cash purchase, with no bridging costs.

1. Buy and refurbish

ItemAmount
Purchase price£120,000
SDLT at higher rates (5% of £120,000)£6,000
Legal, survey and broker fees£2,500
Refurbishment£25,000
Holding costs (council tax, utilities and insurance during works)£2,000
Total cash in£155,500

2. Refinance

ItemAmount
Valuation after works£180,000
New buy-to-let mortgage at 75% LTV£135,000
Less lender arrangement fee−£1,500
Net refinance proceeds£133,500
Money left in (£155,500 − £133,500)£22,000

3. Rent

Annual itemAmount
Rent (£1,100 a month)£13,200
Mortgage interest (interest-only, 5.0% on £135,000)−£6,750
Letting and management (10% + VAT = 12%)−£1,584
Maintenance and voids allowance (8%)−£1,056
Landlord insurance−£400
Safety certificates and compliance (gas, EICR and so on)−£300
Annual pre-tax cash flow£3,110

4. The returns

  • Return on money left in: £3,110 ÷ £22,000 = 14.1%
  • Gross yield on end value: £13,200 ÷ £180,000 = 7.3%
  • Equity created: £180,000 − £135,000 mortgage = £45,000

Stress test: what if rates rise?

At a 7.0% interest rate, mortgage interest rises to £9,450 and cash flow falls to about £410 a year. The deal still washes its face, but only just. Lenders run their own stress tests (an interest cover ratio, often 125%–145% of the payment at a stressed rate), which can limit how much you're able to borrow.

Things the simple maths leaves out

  • Tax: for individual landlords, mortgage interest gets only a basic-rate tax credit (often called "Section 24"), so higher-rate taxpayers keep less. See GOV.UK's guidance.
  • Valuation risk: if the property is valued at £165,000 instead, 75% LTV gives £123,750 and much more of your money stays in the deal.
  • Refurbishment overruns and delays, and lender rules on how soon after purchase you can refinance.
