Pressure testing and leak investigation records for plumbers

Two bits of paperwork come back to bite more often than any other: the pressure test nobody wrote down before the floor went down, and the leak call-out where the insurer later asks exactly what you checked. Here's what I record on both, with worked examples.

Why bother?

The Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999 require water fittings to be installed and tested so they're watertight and fit for purpose. Your contract or the client's specification will normally say which test method and limits apply. A written record shows you did the test, to which method, with what result, before anything was boxed in. Gas tightness testing is separate: it must be done by a registered gas engineer under the procedures that apply to gas work (see HSE: gas safety).

What to put on a pressure test record

  • Job details: site, client, date, engineer.
  • Section or zone tested: for example "first-floor bathroom, hot and cold", with pipe material and jointing method.
  • Test method and limits: the standard, specification or manufacturer's instructions you're working to, the test pressure and the allowed drop. Don't invent limits; copy them from the method.
  • Gauge: ID or serial and the date it was last calibrated or checked.
  • Readings: stabilisation time, test start and end times, start and end pressure, and ambient temperature (temperature changes can move the reading).
  • Press-fit checks: if you used press fittings, the tool, jaw profile and how you checked for unpressed joints.
  • Result: pass or fail against the allowed drop, plus any remedial work and a re-test.
  • Sign-off: your signature, and the client's or site manager's if possible.

A photo of the gauge at the start and end of the test, with the time visible, is cheap insurance.

What to put on a leak investigation record

  • Time the call came in and the time you arrived.
  • What the customer reported, in their words.
  • Meter readings: two readings, the time between them and the flow worked out.
  • Areas checked and how: visual, moisture meter, thermal camera, acoustic, or isolating sections and watching the pressure.
  • What you found, the cause, and whether the repair is temporary or permanent.
  • Damage seen, materials used and photo numbers.
  • Status: resolved, temporary fix or unresolved, plus what happens next.

Worked example: meter flow check

With every tap and appliance off, the meter reads 1,234.567 m³ at 14:00 and 1,234.582 m³ at 14:10. That's 0.015 m³ (15 litres) in 10 minutes, or 1.5 litres per minute. Water is being used when nothing should be, so there's likely a leak after the meter. Isolate sections in turn and repeat to narrow it down.

Write the readings down exactly as shown on the meter. A loss adjuster can check your sums later, but only if the numbers are there.

Tips

  • Fill in the record on site while the gauge is still connected.
  • Number your photos and write the numbers on the sheet.
  • Give the client a copy the same day.
