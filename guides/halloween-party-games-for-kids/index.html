Last-minute Halloween party games for kids (with a free indoor scavenger hunt)

Halloween falls on a Saturday this year, 31 October 2026, so plenty of us will have a houseful of small witches and pumpkins with very little time to plan. These games work indoors, use things you already own and suit mixed ages from about four to eleven.

Before you start: three quick decisions

  • How many children? Up to eight, most games work as one group. Above that, split into two teams and run games in parallel.
  • How old? Under-sixes need short games with no reading. Older children like a challenge and a scoreboard.
  • Prizes or no prizes? A small bowl of wrapped sweets or stickers, with everyone winning something by the end, avoids tears.

Games that need almost nothing

1. Indoor scavenger hunt

Hide ten small things around one or two rooms: a toy spider, an orange sock, a torch, a pumpkin sticker and so on. Give each child (or pair) a list with pictures so non-readers can join in, and let them tick items off. Our free printable Halloween scavenger hunt does the list for you, in A4 and US Letter, colour or ink-saver.

Tip: tell them to find and tick, not collect. Otherwise the first child clears the room and the rest have nothing to find.

2. Mummy wrap race

Pairs or threes, one roll of cheap toilet roll each. One child is the mummy; the others wrap from the ankles up. Stop at the shoulders and keep paper away from faces. Two minutes on the clock, then the grown-up picks the "most mummified".

3. Freeze like a ghost

Musical statues with a theme. Play spooky music; when it stops, everyone freezes in their best ghost pose. Anyone who wobbles becomes a "ghost judge" and helps spot the next wobbler, so nobody sits out for long.

4. Pumpkin spoon relay

Two teams, a dessert spoon each and a satsuma (a perfect mini pumpkin) per team. Walk to a chair and back without dropping it, then hand over. Use plastic eggs or ping-pong balls for younger ones.

5. Guess how many sweets

Fill a clear jar with wrapped sweets and count them first. Each child writes or tells you a guess, and the closest wins the jar or shares it out. It runs quietly in the background while guests arrive.

6. Spooky story circle

Sit in a circle with the lights low and a torch. You start: "On Halloween night, a small cat heard a knock at the door..." Each child adds one sentence, then passes the torch. Keep it silly rather than scary for the younger ones.

Keeping it safe and fun

  • Use battery tea lights rather than real candles in carved pumpkins, especially near costumes.
  • Whole small sweets and nuts can be a choking risk for little ones, so pick age-appropriate treats and check for allergies with parents.
  • Keep hiding places at child height and out of kitchens, stairs and bathrooms.
  • For the very young, "not too spooky" is the rule: bright pumpkins and friendly ghosts, not zombies.

Printing tips

Print at "Actual size" (100%) so nothing gets cropped. Use the ink-saver version if you're printing lots of copies, and thin card (around 160–200 gsm, if your printer takes it) for anything that gets handled, such as bingo or picture cards.
