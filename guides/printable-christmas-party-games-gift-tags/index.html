12 printable Christmas party games for families and office parties (UK)

Good party games need three things: simple rules, a bit of competition and nobody feeling put on the spot. Here are twelve that work at home or at work, how to run them, and a short guide to printing your own gift tags on card.

Games for families (mixed ages)

  1. Who Am I? Stick a card with a Christmas character or object on each player's back or forehead. They ask yes/no questions until they guess it. Try our free printable Who Am I? cards.
  2. Christmas bingo. Fill a grid with festive pictures or words (stocking, robin, sprout, cracker). Call them out at random. Picture cards let younger children play alongside adults.
  3. Wrapping race. Pairs wrap an awkward object, such as a football or a mug, using one hand each. Best-wrapped in two minutes wins.
  4. Christmas charades. Write film, song and activity prompts on slips. Keep them generic ("building a snowman", "carol singing") so everyone gets a fair go.
  5. Pass the parcel with forfeits. Add a forfeit slip in some layers: sing a line of a carol, do your best reindeer impression, tell a cracker joke.
  6. Family Christmas quiz. Four rounds of five questions: traditions, food, music and a picture round. Mix easy and hard so children can score too.

Games for office parties

  1. Christmas trivia teams. Tables of four to six, one answer sheet each. Ten questions per round keeps it moving between courses.
  2. Find someone who... A sheet of prompts ("has been ice skating this year", "can name all of the reindeer"). People mingle and collect names. A good icebreaker for new starters.
  3. Emoji carols. Show a carol title as emojis; teams write their answers. Easy to prepare and no singing required.
  4. Two truths and a festive lie. Each person shares three Christmas facts about themselves; others guess the fib.
  5. Draw it. One person draws a Christmas word, their team guesses within 60 seconds. Works on a whiteboard or on paper.
  6. Secret gift swap with dice. Everyone brings a wrapped gift to an agreed budget. Roll a die: a six means swap with anyone, a one means pass left. Stop after five minutes.

Running it smoothly

  • Pick three or four games for a two-hour party. Twelve is a menu, not a plan.
  • Print answer sheets and a host card in advance. Nobody wants to wait while the quizmaster finds the printer.
  • At work, keep forfeits and questions inclusive, and avoid anything that singles out individuals.

How to print Christmas gift tags on card at home

  1. Check your printer. Look up the heaviest paper it supports, often listed in gsm in the manual. Many home inkjets manage around 200 gsm, some more.
  2. Choose the card. 200–250 gsm matt card feels sturdy and takes ink well. Glossy photo paper smudges more if you handwrite on it.
  3. Set the printer. Choose "Actual size" or 100% scale, the right paper size (A4 or US Letter), and a "card" or "heavy paper" media setting if there is one. Feed a single sheet at a time, via the rear or manual tray if you have one.
  4. Test on plain paper first to check the layout before you use card.
  5. Cut and finish. A craft knife and metal ruler give straighter edges than scissors. Punch a hole about 1 cm from the edge and thread ribbon or baker's twine.
