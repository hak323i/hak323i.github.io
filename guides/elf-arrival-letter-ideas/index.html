Elf arrival ideas: a welcome letter and treasure hunt for the first night

Many families welcome a little Christmas elf on 1 December, or the night before. The arrival sets the tone for the whole month, so here are simple ideas, a letter you can adapt and a treasure hunt that turns the first morning into an adventure.

Arrival ideas that take ten minutes

  • The snowy landing: a little flour or icing sugar on a tray, with tiny footprints drawn in using a fingertip.
  • The breakfast arrival: the elf sits by the cereal bowls with a note and a festive treat.
  • The parcel: the elf arrives in a box tied with string, posted "from the North Pole".
  • The zip wire: string between two chairs, with the elf hanging from a peg as if it slid in overnight.
  • The hunt: no elf in sight, just a letter and a first clue. The elf is found at the end. (More below.)

What to write in the welcome letter

Keep it short enough to read aloud in a minute. A good letter:

  1. introduces the elf by name (pick your own, it's more personal);
  2. says where they've travelled from and why they've come;
  3. sets out the house rules, for example: "Please don't touch me, or my magic might fade";
  4. mentions something specific about the child, such as a new school or a pet;
  5. ends with something to look forward to.

Example: "Dear Maya and Sam, I'm Juniper, and I've flown all the way from the North Pole to spend December with you! I heard Maya started Year 2 and Sam learned to ride his bike, well done! Each night I fly home to report back, and each morning I'll land somewhere new. Please don't touch me, because elf magic is delicate. To find me today, follow the clues. The first is under something you sit on to watch TV... Love, Juniper"

A first-night treasure hunt

Five or six clues is plenty for an excited child before school. Hide them in reverse order, so you finish where you started.

For children under six, use picture clues instead of words. Older children enjoy riddles and a short code to crack at the end.

Keeping it manageable all month

  • Write down 24 easy poses in advance so you aren't inventing one at midnight.
  • Not every day needs a big scene. A new spot and a one-line note is enough.
  • Plan a goodbye letter for Christmas Eve, so the ending is as special as the arrival.
