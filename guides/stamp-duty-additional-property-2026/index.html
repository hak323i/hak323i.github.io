Stamp duty in 2026/27 and the 5% additional property surcharge

If you're buying a buy-to-let or a second home in England or Northern Ireland, Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) can be one of the largest upfront costs. Here are the current rates and how to work out the bill.

Rates checked against GOV.UK: SDLT residential property rates on 10 October 2026. Scotland (LBTT) and Wales (LTT) have their own separate taxes.

Standard residential rates

Portion of the priceStandard rateHigher rate (additional property)
Up to £125,0000%5%
£125,001 to £250,0002%7%
£250,001 to £925,0005%10%
£925,001 to £1.5 million10%15%
Above £1.5 million12%17%

SDLT is charged in slices, like income tax. Each rate applies only to the part of the price that falls within its band.

The 5% higher rates for additional properties

According to GOV.UK, you'll usually pay 5% on top of the standard rates if buying the property means you'll own more than one residential property. This typically covers buy-to-let purchases and second homes. Purchases by companies normally pay the higher rates too, and there are special rules for some corporate purchases. Properties under £40,000 are generally not subject to the higher rates.

If you're replacing your main residence, you won't pay the extra 5% as long as you sell your previous main home. If you complete the new purchase before selling, you pay the higher rates at first, but you may be able to claim a refund if you sell your previous main home within 36 months. See GOV.UK's guidance on additional properties.

Other surcharges and reliefs

  • Non-UK residents: usually pay an extra 2% on top of any other rates that apply.
  • First-time buyers: no SDLT up to £300,000 and 5% on the portion from £300,001 to £500,000. The relief isn't available above £500,000, and it doesn't apply to investors who already own property.

Worked examples (additional property)

For comparison, a £295,000 purchase at standard rates costs £4,750 (GOV.UK's own example), so the surcharge adds £14,750.

Factor it into your deal maths

SDLT goes straight into your total cash in. It isn't usually recovered when you refinance, so it reduces your return. See our BRRR worked example to see the effect. Always confirm your figure with HMRC's SDLT calculator or a conveyancer.
