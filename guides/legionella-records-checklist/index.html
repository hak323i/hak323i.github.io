What legionella records do you need? A practical L8 and HSG274 checklist

If you manage a rental property, block of flats or small workplace, you have a legal duty to assess and control the risk from legionella bacteria. Good records show that the controls are actually being carried out. Here's what to keep.

The legal background in brief

The duty comes from the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH). HSE's Approved Code of Practice L8, Legionnaires' disease: The control of legionella bacteria in water systems sets out what's expected, and the technical guidance HSG274 (Part 2 covers hot and cold water systems) gives the detail. HSE also has specific guidance for landlords.

For most domestic lets, HSE says a simple risk assessment is usually enough and testing for legionella is not normally needed. Larger or more complex systems, such as those with stored hot water, calorifiers, communal tanks or showers in shared buildings, need a fuller written scheme of control and regular monitoring.

Records to keep

L8 says that where you have five or more employees you must record the significant findings of the risk assessment. In practice it's good sense to keep the following for any system you're responsible for:

  • Risk assessment: who did it, when, what they found and when it's due for review.
  • Responsible person: the name of the person appointed to manage the controls.
  • Written scheme of control: what's checked, how often and by whom.
  • Monitoring results: temperatures, dates, who took the readings and any failures.
  • Remedial action: what was done when a check failed, and when.
  • Cleaning and maintenance: descaling showerheads, tank inspections and TMV servicing.

Typical check frequencies (HSG274 Part 2)

CheckTypical frequencyWhat "good" looks like
Hot water storage (calorifier) flow and return temperatureMonthlyFlow at least 60°C, return at least 50°C
Hot water sentinel outletsMonthlyAt least 50°C within one minute of running (55°C in healthcare premises)
Cold water sentinel outletsMonthlyBelow 20°C within two minutes of running
Little-used outletsWeekly flushingRun until the water is at its normal temperature, then record it
Showerheads and hosesQuarterly (or as the risk assessment says)Cleaned, descaled and disinfected
Cold water storage tanksAt least annuallyInspected for condition and debris, with temperatures recorded
Risk assessment reviewRegularly, and whenever something changesReviewed and dated

Your own risk assessment may set different frequencies. Follow it, and record why.

How long to keep records

L8 says monitoring records should be kept for at least five years. Keep them somewhere they can be shown easily to an inspector, insurer or managing agent.

Tips for records that hold up

  • Record the actual reading, not just "OK".
  • Make failures obvious, and log the corrective action next to them.
  • Keep a fixed list of sentinel outlets so readings can be compared month to month.
  • Back up digital records, and date and sign or initial each entry.
