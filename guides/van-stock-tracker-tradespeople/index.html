How to keep track of van stock as a plumber or electrician

Running out of a 15 mm elbow halfway through a job costs far more than the elbow. A trip to the merchant can lose you an hour. Here's a simple system that keeps the van stocked without tying up cash in parts you never use.

Step 1: do one honest stocktake

Empty one section of the van at a time, not the whole thing. Note each item, the quantity and roughly what you paid. Bin or return anything damaged, obsolete or that you haven't touched in a year. Most people find a surprising amount of money sitting in the back.

Step 2: set minimum and maximum levels

For each item, decide the fewest you'd want on board (the minimum) and how many you'd fill up to (the maximum). A useful starting point:

  • Minimum = what you'd use in a typical week, plus a few for the unexpected.
  • Maximum = roughly two weeks' use, or a merchant pack size.

The rule: when stock is at or below the minimum, order enough to get back to the maximum. In a spreadsheet that's =IF(OnVan<=Min, Max-OnVan, 0), which turns your stock list into a shopping list.

Step 3: a 15-minute weekly routine

  1. Friday afternoon, count only the fast movers. The slow ones can wait for a monthly check.
  2. Update the sheet and let it build the order.
  3. Compare prices across your usual merchants after your trade discount.
  4. Order for Monday collection or delivery.

Step 4: register your tools

Van break-ins are a real risk for trades. Keep a list of every power tool and expensive hand tool with make, model, serial number, purchase date, price and a photo. If something is stolen, you can give the police and your insurer a full list straight away. Marking tools with a UV pen or engraving your postcode helps too.

Step 5: tool checks

Under the work equipment regulations (PUWER), HSE expects equipment to be suitable, maintained and inspected where wear could make it unsafe. A simple visual check log, with the date, the tool, what you checked and the next check due, is easy to add to the same spreadsheet. For portable electrical equipment, HSE's guidance is that the frequency of checks depends on the risk, so set it yourself rather than assuming a fixed annual test.

Tips from the van

  • Label the shelves and boxes; it makes counting faster than any app.
  • Keep a "job-specific" crate separate from core stock, so a big bathroom fit doesn't eat your everyday parts.
  • Return unused parts to the merchant promptly while they'll still take them back.
