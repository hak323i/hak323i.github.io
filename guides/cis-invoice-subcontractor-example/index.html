CIS invoices for subcontractors: what to include, with an example

Under the Construction Industry Scheme (CIS), the contractor takes tax off your labour before paying you. Your invoice needs to make that easy to calculate. Get the split wrong and you'll either be underpaid or chasing corrections.

Sources: GOV.UK: What you must do as a CIS subcontractor, GOV.UK: CIS contractors and GOV.UK: VAT domestic reverse charge. Checked 10 October 2026.

The deduction rates

  • 20% if you're registered for CIS
  • 30% if you're not registered
  • 0% if HMRC has given you gross payment status

The deduction applies to your labour and other work, not to VAT or to the cost of materials you bought for the job. GOV.UK also lists equipment or plant hire, consumable stores and fuel (other than for travel) as costs the contractor can take out before working out the deduction.

What to put on the invoice

  1. Your name, address and invoice number, and the date.
  2. The contractor's name and address.
  3. Your Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR), so the contractor can match you to their verification.
  4. A description of the work and the site.
  5. Labour and materials shown separately, with materials at cost.
  6. The CIS rate and the amount deducted.
  7. VAT treatment: standard rate, reduced rate, or the domestic reverse charge wording. Add your VAT number if registered.
  8. The amount due and your payment details.

Worked example (VAT registered, reverse charge)

A plumbing subcontractor, registered for CIS and VAT, works three days on a new-build for a VAT-registered contractor. The reverse charge applies, so the contractor accounts for the VAT.

LineAmount
Labour: 3 days at £220£660.00
Materials (paid by me, at cost)£214.40
Net total£874.40
VAT at 20% (£174.88): reverse charge, customer to account to HMRC£0.00 charged
Less CIS deduction: 20% of £660.00 labour−£132.00
Amount due£742.40

The invoice should state the reverse charge clearly, for example: "Reverse charge: customer to account to HMRC for the VAT". GOV.UK's guidance gives example wording.

Same job, not registered for CIS

If the subcontractor wasn't registered, the deduction rises to 30% of £660 = £198.00. If they also weren't VAT registered, there would be no VAT line, and the amount due would be £874.40 − £198.00 = £676.40.

The domestic reverse charge doesn't apply when the customer is an end user or an intermediary supplier (GOV.UK defines an end user as a business that receives the work but does not make an onward supply of it, and an intermediary supplier as a business connected or linked to an end user). If the customer has told you in writing that they are one of these, charge VAT in the normal way. See GOV.UK for the definitions.

After you're paid

The contractor must give you a payment and deduction statement within 14 days of the end of each tax month (tax months run from the 6th to the 5th). Keep every statement. Your deductions count towards your tax and National Insurance bill when you do your Self Assessment return, so missing statements can mean paying twice.

Common mistakes

  • Lumping labour and materials into one "supply and fix" figure, so the contractor deducts 20% of everything.
  • Charging VAT on a reverse charge job, or applying the reverse charge to a job that's for the end user.
  • Not chasing missing monthly statements.
