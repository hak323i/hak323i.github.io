How to use now/next boards and visual schedules at home or school

A now/next board shows two things: what's happening now and what comes next. A visual schedule shows more of the day. Many children and adults, including lots of autistic people, find that seeing the plan makes the day easier to follow, and it saves a lot of repeating yourself. Here's how to set one up so it actually gets used.

Now/next or a full schedule?

Now/next boardVisual schedule
ShowsTwo stepsSeveral steps, part of a day or a whole day
Good forGetting started, a busy moment, a less-preferred task followed by a favourite oneRoutines such as mornings and bedtime, school days, trips out
Set-up timeSecondsA few minutes

Many families use both: a schedule for the day and a now/next board for the moment in front of them.

Getting started

  1. Pick the right kind of picture. Photos, symbols or words all work. Use whatever the person recognises and likes. Always add a word label, so everyone uses the same name for things.
  2. Start with something positive. Introduce the board when things are calm, with a "next" that's enjoyable: "Now shoes, next park."
  3. Keep the language short and the same each time. Point to each card as you say it.
  4. Follow through. If the board says park next, go to the park. The board only works if it can be trusted.
  5. Show when something's finished. Turn the card over, move it to a "done" pocket or post it in an envelope.

Worked example: a school morning

StepCardTip
1ToiletStart with something quick and familiar
2Get dressedLay the clothes out in order the night before
3BreakfastA chosen breakfast card gives some control
4Brush teethA timer card helps if this one drags
5Shoes and coatPut the coat card next to the door as well
6Car or walk to schoolAdd a "favourite song" card for the journey if it helps

Six steps is plenty to start with. If the whole morning feels like too much, show three at a time and swap them as you go.

Worked example: a day out with a change of plan

Plans change: the park is shut, or the bus is late. Keep a "change" card (a simple symbol or the word "change") and use it calmly: "Change. Park is closed. Now café, next playground." Practise using it for small, positive changes first, so it isn't only ever linked with disappointment.

At school

  • A desk strip with four or five steps is easier to follow than a big wall chart across the room.
  • Use the same words and pictures at home and school if you can. Share a few cards with the class teacher.
  • Include break time, lunch and home time, so the end of the day is visible.

Making cards last

Print at 100% (actual size) so the cards fit the pockets. Laminating and a hook-and-loop dot on the back of each card and pocket makes them easy to move. Keep spares of the cards you use daily.
